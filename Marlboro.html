Growing up in town, everyone knew the Piastri Family.

 

They had mafia ties running back to the sixties. It was the type of generational wealth that showed in their outfits and accessories. Knuckles lined with glittering diamond rings, gold watches, tattoos that peeked at you beneath the cotton of their silk button ups. 

 

It wasn’t the money that intimidated people, though. It was the influence.

 

If Chris Piastri liked a restaurant, it flourished. Lines around the block and reservations up the wazoo. If his wife Nicole decided she wasn’t a fan of a particular bakery, it would be deserted for weeks before eventually shutting its doors. If one of their four kids frequented a beach, all of a sudden it was the new hot spot for the summer.

 

They were friends with the politicians and the police. They helped fund the local museums and libraries, but they also lurked in all the darkest alleyways. The townspeople both admired and feared the Piastris, but one thing was certain–they ran the town. That was how it had always been, and how it would always be.

 

Lando’s first run in with the family didn’t happen until he was nineteen years old. He was a sophomore in college, in the middle of one of his hardest semesters so far. (Though he did have a habit of saying that about all of them.)

 

His parents owned a few gas stations around town, and he liked to do his part and help out by standing in whenever he was needed. It usually ended up being a night shift. To be honest, Lando hated them. He was a big partier, having been an extrovert for as long as he could remember. Working a night shift was a guaranteed way to keep him from going out.

 

He’d started to wonder if that was intentional on his dad’s part.

And end this before it went any further.
