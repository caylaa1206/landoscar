This week's golf session hadn't exactly been relaxing.

With the sharp swish of a club slicing through the air, Ria hit the stop button on the camera and gave Lando a thumbs-up. That was the content done for the day. Max, sitting in the back seat, continued to pontificate loudly into his phone, not putting it down even as they walked into the restaurant.

Lando listened in silence, realizing exactly who was on the other end of the line.

As a gaming streamer trying to pivot into a personal brand, Lando and his small workshop of a team were already burnt out by the workload. They had long since stopped updating long-form YouTube videos or stream highlights. Noticing the gap, quite a few fans had taken the initiative to do it for him, bringing in a wave of new followers, which Lando was happy to sit back and enjoy.

Among them, one clipper stood out. Their profile picture was an apple pie with sprinkles, their view counts were exceptionally high, and their upload schedule was terrifyingly consistent. Even when Lando was in a slump, broadcasting sessions of Forza Horizon 5 that could put an insomniac to sleep, this editor managed to cut them into videos with actual narrative arcs.

The killer part? This channel didn’t monetize with ads and never interacted in the increasingly crowded comment section. It wasn’t until the name "81Pastry" started appearing constantly on the lips of his regulars—and people started digging for a nonexistent relationship between him and Lando—that the team finally realized who was stealing their viewership.

After all, everyone is busy. For some fans, once they realize they can watch a 30-minute edit instead of the streamer driving a Subaru for 10 hours, there seems little reason to enter the livestream when they just want some quiet entertainment.

The backend data from YouTube and Twitch was too complex for Lando. To him, the line graphs and bar charts just went up and down, trading places. Later, Max claimed it was because they wasted too much time clearing out the digital cobwebs from their hard drives. During the busy period of registering the company, they returned to their screens and webcams, only to miss the initial hype of the new Monster Hunter. "For me, I have to know."

"I knew it!"

I was wondering how you knew since you had just bought the car yourself... You must have been really fast back then..." He mused aloud. "Can I still find media interviews from that time? Where were you when we were recruiting drivers at Silverstone?"

Instinct can't fix this gearbox, nor can it make those brands look at me with any more respect. The reason they issue me VIP passes is because I'm a useful display window."

He took a deep breath, trying to forcefully wrench the topic away from his embarrassing ancient history. But when he saw Oscar had already taken off his gloves and was genuinely about to fish out his phone to search for these so-called "racing records," a panic of being stripped bare made him disregard everything else. He lunged forward and pinned Oscar's hand down.

Knocked half a step back by the impact, Oscar saw how serious he was, shrugged, and pulled his hand back with a regretful look, though his eyes were clearly still plotting something.

"When I first started making videos, the people I knew from the karting teams were all saying, 'Since he can't be a driver, being an influencer isn't bad.' Every sentence sounded on the verge of his voice cracking. Like I'm somewhat smart, and maybe even worthy of respect."

If even their very reason for standing here fixing a car was based on a massive online carnival, was his pitiful pride also just a scam existing solely on a Premiere Pro timeline? Having said this much, he actually wanted to continue with bold proclamations: Because you're here, I want to win something more than ever before. "Why are you still calling it an illusion? Editing isn't magic. I can't fabricate things you never did out of thin air."

Lando peeked up, crashing into Oscar's gaze of disbelief. It was as if, in Oscar's eyes, everything was simply a given. Lando had been saved by this attitude too many times. Oscar could always fish him out of an emotional quagmire from the most unexpected angle; perhaps that was the real magic. Wind blew into the garage, and Lando actually shivered as it swept across his sweat-soaked back. He wanted to say something in response, but this time his throat was truly blocked. Physically unable to speak, he could only raise a trembling hand to wipe his forehead. It wasn't until the V8 engine finally burst into its first cough that viewers in front of their screens realized they had been holding their breath the whole time.

Fans bounced back and forth between the two videos, trying to piece together the full picture of the car's resurrection. This meant double the clicks and a higher completion rate. The office air was thick with an overpowering smell of coffee. When he swiped his card to enter the building, the intense sense of disconnect almost gave him vertigo. "Oh, I thought you were transferring from Melbourne. That headquarters finally took pity on us," the colleague continued on his own. "We practically got exiled here. We can't even spare the hands to recruit newbies."

He deliberately emphasized the word "exiled." As a fellow Aussie, Oscar briefly expressed appreciation for this "reverse exile" humor. He greeted a few others in the office, enduring their complaints for a long time while his phone vibrated in his pocket, before finally managing to escape.

Finally free from his colleagues' small talk, Oscar stepped out of the revolving doors, stood beneath the grayish sky, and let out a long breath. That oppressive smell of coffee finally dissipated, replaced by a damp, cool breeze and a faint scent of cigarette smoke.

That explained why his phone hadn't stopped buzzing; clearly, the other recipient was reacting faster than he was.

Oscar's finger hovered over the screen for a second. He knew this was the best opportunity this adventure could bring him, but also the one with the slimmest odds.

He tapped the email open. The first paragraph was standard PR rhetoric: the other party politely and regretfully stated that Porsche's budget for the classic car department at Goodwood was already locked in, and they had to use the materials produced by their ad agency and the pre-decided guests no matter what. Oscar actually felt slightly relieved; it was an expected blow. Yet, he instinctively kept reading.

"However," the twist appeared in the second paragraph, "Mark is personally very interested in your video. He said your perspective in the edit reminded him of what it was like attending team seminars back in the day. If you're in London this week, perhaps we could schedule a time. Mark is looking for an independent creator for a special curated segment at the Goodwood Revival in September."

Oscar leaned against the cold brick wall of the office building, staring at the bolded words "independent creator." A tight sensation crawled up his spine, making him feel like he was back waking up on cardboard boxes in the Silverstone workshop—his whole body aching, but his mind incredibly sharp. This was just another... slightly more impressive interview opportunity. It still didn't provide a badge that could get him into the Goodwood venue. He took a deep breath and opened WhatsApp.

"Did you see it!!!!"

"I told you he'd love it!"

The sheer volume of exclamation marks and emojis left Oscar quite overwhelmed. There was also a photo: Lando at a train station wearing a cap and sunglasses, with a train timetable to Blackpool in the background.

"I'm heading out to deal with those old guys at TVR. Even though they might be too broke to even buy me a cup of tea, I'm still gonna try with my charming smile."

"Keep your back straight when you go meet Mark."

"Grab an autograph for me while you're at it? I need to check if I still have a Red Bull cap at home."

Oscar stared at the screen. Anxious office workers hurried past him on the street, barking into their phones, but all he saw were Lando's messages full of all-caps letters and exclamation marks. His legs suddenly felt a bit weak. Hugging the heavy backpack that contained his entire livelihood, he slowly slid down the rough brick wall into a crouch.

He buried his face in the backpack straps, taking a deep breath of the cold air tainted with rain and exhaust fumes. Fear hit him belatedly—not just because Mark Webber actually wanted to meet him, but because all of this was truly starting to turn with an astonishing heaviness. He might not have been ready to rip his hard-earned safety net to shreds, but he simply couldn't stop his hands from pushing more chips onto the poker table. His colleague from earlier was wrong. This wasn't an exile. This was a long-premeditated prison break, one that even he himself was terrified enough to have trembling hands over.

In this little corner that belonged only to him, Oscar lifted his head and woke up the screen again. He didn't text back; he merely reacted with an emoji to almost every message, then exited the chat.

Behind a sparse array of apps, his phone wallpaper was now a pixelated, blurry photo clearly cropped from a scan of some old karting magazine. In the photo was a boy who looked no older than twelve or thirteen, wearing a racing suit two sizes too big. His hair was a mess, but he was hugging a trophy nearly half his size tightly against his chest. His eyes crinkled into a smile at the camera, looking purely happy just for the trophy. Lando had begged him not to search for it. To stop him, Lando had even voluntarily opened his trophy cabinet, trying to use that cold metal to prove it was a dead glory. But what Oscar wanted to do was not up to anyone else.

Oscar looked at the boy's proud face on the screen for a moment longer. This photo wouldn't lie.

Editing isn't magic. I can't fabricate things you never did. He remembered blurting that out to Lando the other day. Honestly, he was still feeling a lingering fear over his arrogant declarations about "winning" when they were packing up the tent. But I know you've won before, which is why I dare to stay on the table. He recited to himself. He locked his phone, struggled up from the ground alone, and slung the backpack over his shoulder. The newly acquired Mac Studio dug painfully into his shoulder, but he felt it was probably the lightest thing he had ever carried in his life.

Just make the initial rev climb steep, then smooth it out and cap it so it doesn't actually spin out... But he just told me to stop doing charity for poms."

"Poms... Should I not ask about your little inside jokes?" Lando teased, his tone dipping into jealousy. Oscar could tell Lando was genuinely worried about being left on the outside.

"I'm not doing charity for you either," Oscar joked, nodding toward the wallet with the massive check. "So I just played dumb. I told him I tune road cars, so I map for safety. Plus, I haven't touched their transmission control unit, and with only a few days and a prototype, there's only so much I can do—sorry, that's all the help you get."

"But you could actually make it perfect?"

Oscar nodded, giving a helpless shrug and casting another glance at the wallet.

"You've really learned the ropes," Lando mused. "Remind me never to cross you..."

He broke off, suddenly remembering something. He stood up, rummaged through his backpack, and pulled out a much simpler set of keys. Oscar instantly recognized them—they belonged to the now-legendary Elise S1.

"I got it," Lando said. "Since you're such a terrifying opponent, consider this my bribe... I talked to the track marshals. We get the track for thirty minutes Saturday morning. No speed limits, no rules. You can drive it however you want, just don't crash it. It’s what I promised you. No sleeping in."

"Isn't qualifying on Saturday? Why not Friday?"

"It's going to rain tomorrow," Lando said honestly, watching Oscar drop his fork onto the table and bury his face in the sofa cushions; tuning the Griffith in the rain had clearly traumatized him. "Plus, they modified the Elise to be even more aggressive than it was at Silverstone. It’s down to 680 kilos and up to 210 horsepower. Give me another day to get used to it."

Hearing those specs, Oscar uncovered his eyes, looking at him with genuine concern.

"Are you going to be okay?" Oscar asked. "210 horsepower pushing 680 kilos puts the power-to-weight ratio close to a GT3. I checked the time attack list; if you keep it at 190 horsepower, you’ll probably land around 20th and easily make Sunday’s shootout... I’m not saying don't push, the faster the better, but you were exhausted last time..."

"I'll be fine. Last time at Silverstone was because I did too many laps back-to-back," Lando dismissed with a wave. "The hill climb is less than two kilometers. It’s just one minute of pure focus. It's over before you even feel tired."

"The shootout is a minute, but you have a full day of practice on Friday and qualifying on Saturday." Oscar wasn't buying it. "To handle that horsepower, Lotus must have set up a violent front toe angle. On Goodwood's surface, that steering wheel is going to kick back hard enough to snap your wrists."

He sat up, his tone shifting into a serious pre-race briefing.

"If you burn through your energy during Friday's practice, your forearm muscles will be too stiff to feel the grip changes by Sunday's final. That's where the danger is, Lando. Fatigue makes you slow, and the Elise relies entirely on corner speed. It doesn't take hesitation. You either make it through, or you end up in the wall."

"How do you know all this so well?" Lando interrupted.

Oscar sighed, rolling his eyes slightly.

"You literally know I built and drove those uni student formula cars. What did you think? We used motorcycle engines to push—listen to this—a 170-kilo chassis. No power steering, no suspension bushings, nothing."

Lando blinked, then mouthed quietly, I didn't go to a uni.

"Fine, I'll save my energy on Friday. Just map the track and braking points, then come back." He nodded, watching Oscar go back to his food. A slight chill ran through him. If Oscar hadn't gone the academic route and had chosen to race instead, any junior driver wanting a title would have to pray they never shared a grid with him. Lando even started calculating his own racing years. Of course, that assumed Oscar wouldn't have faced the same bad luck he did.

Friday's rain arrived exactly as predicted, drizzling all day. The Lotus team kept things cautious, and Lando kept his promise—walking the track under an umbrella with his engineer and keeping the Elise's roof firmly on during practice. It wasn't until Saturday morning that the rain finally stopped.

Goodwood hadn't fully woken up yet. A thick fog blanketed the grass, and before the crowds brought the noise, the nearby woods echoed with morning birdsong. Supercars of every shape slept under covers in the paddock; the air smelled of damp earth. The Elise S1 sat at the pit lane exit, its stance even wilder than at Silverstone, a bright flash of color in the gray mist.

Lando tossed the keys to Oscar, who slid into the cramped cockpit. After buckling his helmet and harness, Oscar shot Lando a questioning look, then glanced at the passenger seat.

"I'll skip this run. Extra weight changes the balance," Lando said. "Besides, it’s in much better shape than the Esprit. You've got this."

The engine roared to life, echoing across the empty track, sounding rougher and rawer than it had at Silverstone. Oscar engaged the gear and hit the throttle. Leaning against the barrier, Lando watched the yellow car disappear into the first corner. He could hear Oscar driving conservatively—not hunting for lap times, but evaluating the build from an engineer’s perspective, scanning every mechanical joint for any unrefined friction.

Five minutes later, the Elise pulled up smoothly in front of Lando. Oscar cut the engine but stayed inside, rolling down the window. Realizing his helmet made it impossible to look up and talk comfortably, he unbuckled and climbed out.

"I don't know if you felt it yesterday, but there’s about five millimeters of dead zone at the top of the throttle pedal," Oscar said, his expression grim, looking entirely unlike someone who had just received a bribe. "It’s subtle, but it creates roughly a twenty-millisecond lag when you feed the power back in on corner exit... Don't worry about the exact numbers, that's just what the simulator would show."

"Yeah, there is a bit, but I was taking it slow the last few days so I didn't think much of it."

Oscar pointed to the cabin, gesturing for Lando to get in and test the pedal. The moment Lando pressed it down, he knew Oscar was right. That dead zone hadn't been there at Silverstone. Over the past few days, he had just written the pedal feel off as a quirk of the modifications.

"They put it there on purpose. They gave you 210 horsepower but got scared you couldn't handle it on a narrow track like Goodwood, so they sneaked in a physical limit."

"To keep me from hitting a wall?" Lando understood immediately, shrugging. "I'm out of practice, and Goodwood is incredibly bumpy. It makes sense they'd play it safe."

"But it’s going to hold you back once the pace picks up. I'm taking it out."

Oscar took off his helmet, set it on the driver's seat, and pulled a worn multi-tool card from his pocket. Kneeling on the tarmac, he dove upper-body first under the steering wheel.

"Hey, Osc," Lando looked down at his ungraceful posture, unable to resist teasing him. "How long has it been since you complained about hating tuning cars for delusional rich kids? Forty-eight hours? Didn't you say looking for safety in a sports car was total bullshit? Looks like you're tuning a rich kid's car right now."

The Elise’s footwell offered miserable clearance, and Oscar struggled to tighten the bolt. He froze for a second at the jab, then his muffled voice drifted out, carrying a mix of irritation and hidden indulgence.

"Yeah. Except I'm not adding safety to your car."

A sharp metallic click echoed—the physical limit was gone. Oscar hauled himself out, his hair a messy bird’s nest, still clutching the tool card.

"Done," he said, holding onto the barrier to stand up and brushing the dirt off his trousers. "It’s a pure mechanical connection now. If your foot twitches, the throttle twitches. This is the zero-compromise car you wanted."

Lando stared at the calm face in front of him, fully aware of what this meant. The Goodwood hill climb was tight, bumpy, and lined with unforgiving flint walls and hay bales. Removing that five-millimeter safety net meant that a single moment of hesitation in a corner would send the Elise straight into the wall; but it also meant he wouldn't have to think about managing the lag. He could just go flat out and leave a specially modified Alfa Romeo in his dust.

"Osc, you're trying to kill me."

The air grew still. Oscar didn't look away, nor did he deny the danger. He just picked up his helmet, but right before climbing back in, he leaned close to Lando’s ear and whispered the one spell no racing driver could resist:

"Or I can make you faster."

A chill ran down Lando's spine—the thrill of being completely read. Oscar hadn't just stripped away the car’s safety net; he had demolished Lando's own psychological escape route. He didn't need safety; he needed raw speed, and the guy who could give it to him.

"Go do a few more laps," Lando said, his throat dry with excitement, not fear. "I need to make sure you didn't break it."

When Oscar dropped the clutch and blasted back onto the track, Lando finally heard the tires screaming against the asphalt. Oscar violently tested the car's responsiveness against a driver’s reflexes, like a blacksmith testing a blade's edge before handing it over—ensuring it was lethal, but entirely under control.

A few laps later, the car pulled in. Oscar climbed out, patted the hot hood, and stripped off his gloves and helmet. The serious engineer was gone, replaced by a kid grinning with excitement.

"Now that's a car," Oscar said, his cheeks flushed from focus. "It's all yours. Don't embarrass yourself on the hill."

People were starting to filter onto the track, and voices drifted from the distance. The test run was meant to be a bribe, but Oscar had instinctively turned it into a proper setup session. Since returning from Silverstone, Lando still hadn't asked the question he wanted to ask most: Am I fast enough? Am I fast enough to make you forget that failure in the past? The shootout was still a day away, and asking for validation felt too embarrassing. Besides, plenty of things already proved Oscar wouldn't give him a negative answer.

He looked toward the woods as the fog lifted, his mind drifting. Oscar followed his gaze.

"What are you looking at?" Behind him was just the Elise and the fresh hay bales stacked the night before.

"I just remembered a movie," Lando said. "An old one, with the guy from Notting Hill... Hugh Grant. I can't remember it well... did they escape to a place called Goodwood at the end? I remember the poster—a big estate, thick fog, two guys on horses..."

"No way. I know the movie you mean, but it's definitely not called Goodwood."

Oscar pulled out his phone and quickly searched it under Lando’s gaze. "Maurice, 1987. That is old... See, it’s Greenwood. And the two guys on the poster aren't the ones who escape together. I remember it because Hattie put it on for family movie night."

"Greenwood... sounds close enough to Goodwood. Like a football player's name."

Lando glanced at the screen and offered a weak laugh. He only remembered Hugh Grant playing a nobleman. Scrolling down the Wikipedia page, he realized Grant wasn't the protagonist who suffered through the film and vanished with the man who climbed through his window; he was the one who shut the window, stayed in his mansion, and lived a conventional life.

"Lando?"

Oscar cut into his thoughts, noticing his face cloud over. Of course you know what I'm thinking.

Without a word, Lando pulled Oscar into a tight embrace right there at the edge of the quiet paddock. He felt Oscar wrap his arms around his back, giving his neck a reassuring tap. On Friday, Lando had heard people whispering that Webber was going to steal Oscar away—bring him into the Aussie camp, or take him to Germany or Italy, to some formula team a younger Oscar could only dream of.

After agonizing over that fear for seconds, the morning sun brought back the calm he had felt during their first drive in the Esprit. Even if Oscar was only here for the thrill, even if he was just using him to reach the razzle-dazzle, the time they spent together was reward enough. When Oscar had handed him his driver's license wrapped in that lens cloth, it was the same as him kneeling on the tarmac to eliminate the throttle lag. This was probably the most complicated relationship in the world, bringing out the most intense love and cold ambition all at once. Everyone thought they couldn't balance it, but without a written contract, only a fierce, unyielding blind faith could keep the scales from tipping.

"See you on the hill," Oscar said simply as they parted.

The Griffith was scheduled for an earlier demo run and wouldn't risk the timed shootout, so Oscar could collect the telemetry data and meet him at the top. When Lando finally sat at the start line, the cockpit baking under the sun, Oscar’s prediction came true. He didn't need to think to manage the lag; the car responded instantly, acting as an extension of his own limbs.

The car was so light it felt like it was flying over the uneven surface, every bump violent through the unassisted steering wheel, vibrating through Lando's wrists. Molecomb corner was coming up—the most dangerous spot on the track, a blind left-hander on a downhill slope. Any mistake here meant the flint wall or the hay bales. He smashed the brakes before the turn. Without ABS, the pedal felt like solid stone, tires shrieking at the edge of lockup. The nose sliced perfectly into the apex, clipping the inside grass.

Too fast.

For a split second, logic told him he had carried too much speed, the rear was washing out, and he was about to oversteer. Yesterday, in the car with the five-millimeter lag, he would have crashed. But before his brain could process the danger, his body reacted. He made a microscopic adjustment to the throttle—utilizing that exact primitive range Oscar had just freed up. The power response was instantaneous. The rear tires bit into the asphalt, launching the chassis forward like a scalpel cutting through the corner, brushing past the outside hay bales.

Hearing his side mirror scrape the hay, Lando laughed aloud inside his helmet. The rest of the run was pure adrenaline. The flint wall blurred past on his left. He ran dangerously close to the rough stones, exactly the kind of high-stakes drama the crowd loved and the ultimate test of nerve. He left nothing on the table, discarding any thoughts of public relations damage control if he crashed.

He knew the engineer who had given him that throttle safety net must be turning pale. Their only rule was simple: don't crash. Just making it through to Sunday's shootout would have been a victory. But that would be boring. He pulled into the assembly area and pulled off his helmet, sweat stinging his eyes. He didn't need the timing board to know he was fast, but a sub-60 second run blew his expectations away. Only a few GT4 cars sat ahead of him.

The moment the times went up, reporters carrying Goodwood microphones and cameras swarmed him. That near-miss at Molecomb and his blistering pace made him the man of the hour.

"Lando! That run through Molecomb! How did you pull that off? We saw the rear completely break loose on the monitors. It looked exactly like your Silverstone show!"

"Show?" Lando pushed a bottle of water away, hiding his shaking hands behind his back and flashing his signature grin. "If I wanted to put on a show, I would've knocked the mirror clean off. This is qualifying; I was hunting for pace."

"The Elise S1 has zero electronic assists, and the power-to-weight ratio for this hill climb is terrifying," another reporter interjected. "Without ABS or traction control, how did you find the nerve to brake that late into a blind corner?"

"Because the car does exactly what I tell it to do," Lando said, giving the camera a knowing look. It was time to say it. "My... my mechanic, he cut away the safety net right before the start. Usually, with this much power, teams leave a bit of throttle lag to play it safe, but he took it out. He knew I needed direct feedback."

"That's an incredibly bold setup choice. The Lotus team must have massive faith in you."

"Yeah," Lando lied smoothly, his heart hammering against his ribs. "He knows what I want. If you hesitate in that corner, or if the car hesitates, it's over... It makes the car much harder to drive, but it made me faster. Corner speed is the only way this car can find time."

Amid the media chaos, a hand clapped his shoulder. Lando turned to see Carlos standing behind him in his team kit.

"Brilliant lap, Lando," Carlos congratulated him with a smile. "I knew you'd come back to real driving eventually."

"Thanks... yeah... it was a last-minute decision."

"Last-minute? I wondered why Lotus announced you so late. Why the Elise, though? It’s hardly a typical hill climb car."

Lando scratched his head, unable to explain the truth here. He excused himself, saying they could chat at the bottom of the hill, dodged the Lotus marketing team, and scanned the grandstands. The spectator area at the top was small, but Oscar was nowhere to be seen. A middle-aged man called out to him.

"Hey, you looking for Piastri?"

Lando recognized him as one of the TVR engineers—the one who had glared at him at Silverstone, though he seemed friendlier now.

"Yeah. Do you know where he is?"

"He didn't take the shuttle up with us," the engineer said. "Mark Webber came looking for him again today. Our garage is a bit tight for those two, and I guess he didn't care much about watching qualifying anyway. Did he say he was coming up?"

Before Lando could answer, Max pushed through the crowd, pointing excitedly at the staging area.

"Fifth in the modified production group, Lando! Fifth! You'll be starting near the back tomorrow! Stay in this pace and you're guaranteed a top ten finish! The Lotus guys want a photo, what are you doing over here?"

"He's looking for Piastri. I just told him the kid never came up the hill."

"What are you doing? Find him later, nothing is that urgent right now. For all we know, he's negotiating his salary with Porsche. I told you from the start he wanted—"

"I know."

Lando calmly cut off their bickering. It took an immense amount of effort to maintain his blind faith, but he was holding his ground, surprising even himself. The words he had just given the media would probably cause trouble with the Lotus engineers, but the secrets, the unspoken promises, and the embarrassing questions would all dissolve into the green trees lining the track. At least now I'm a driver who deserves you.

"He must be busy. Just tell Oscar I was looking for him... If you see him."

He flashed a smile, took the water from Max, and trotted back toward the assembly area.

"Whatever you want, you'll get it. Maybe not right now... but it's just a matter of time. Because that's the kind of person you are: when you want something, you just go fight for it." Lando paused. "Funny enough, we're exactly the same. Good night. And good luck tomorrow."

As the call ended, Oscar stopped in his tracks outside the garage. A few engineers still working inside asked where he was going. He made up an excuse about going to the restroom and walked along the pit lane until he reached the deserted trackside in the cool summer night air. He leaned his hands on the barrier, staring at the nearby woods. The moonlight illuminated the hay bales, and the call of an owl drifted from afar. The wind carried the remaining smell of gasoline and the earthy scent of dew on the grass. He must have been hallucinating, because his arms and back ached as if he had been the one tortured by that yellow machine, and his nose was full of the pungent smell of muscle rub.

He let go of the barrier, turned around, and leaned his back against it, listening to the crackling sound of peeling paint falling to the ground. He knew he couldn't stay here long, but he genuinely had too much to digest. He slapped his cheeks, trying to muster the energy to head back inside, but the sound of footsteps on the grass broke his train of thought. A tall, lean figure approached from the direction of the garages. In the cloudless moonlight, Oscar recognized George's face.

"They said you went to the restroom?"

"...Obviously I didn't."

Under George's scrutinizing gaze, Oscar quickly adjusted his expression and stood up straight.

"I just came from the Cartier Lawn... Carlos invited us, but Lando didn't show up, so I went to ask Max what happened." George walked up and leaned casually against the barrier as well, looking toward the closed garage doors in the pit lane. Suddenly, he glanced at Oscar and abruptly changed the subject. "Do you know how the Elise got its name?"

Oscar shook his head. George looked quite pleased to be able to teach him something new.

"Mid-90s. Lotus was on the verge of bankruptcy. Romano Artioli made a desperate bet and named the car after his granddaughter," George said, as if talking to himself. "It was their last gamble. If it failed, the Lotus brand would be gone. It saved the company from the cliff edge—extreme simplicity, extreme lightweight. Maybe that's why they gave it a child's name... returning a car to what it originally was."

Oscar's throat tightened. He didn't know how much George knew, but he understood the metaphor.

"He really wants to win," Oscar said quietly. "More than anyone."

"I know he wants to win. I've known him since he was driving go-karts. For him, I'm willing to play both sides, help him get those Glastonbury tickets, and come here to interrogate you." George's tone suddenly sharpened. "Because I also know what kind of person he is. Once he decides to commit to something, he holds nothing back—whether it's racing or a person. I was at the assembly area at the top of the hill when qualifying ended today, Oscar. You know what I mean."

His sharp tone forced Oscar to summon some courage to look back into those blue eyes.

"So I need to ask you straight, Oscar. Are you looking for an exit strategy? Once Goodwood is over and you get the resume bullet or whatever it is you want, are you going to go back to Australia, or go to Germany, or Italy, or anywhere else better suited for someone 'born for the track' like you? Just like Carlos ultimately decided to follow his father's footsteps and become a proper rally driver?"

This wasn't a casual chat between friends; this was an interrogation. The one thing George couldn't tolerate was history repeating itself, and clearly, in his eyes, if Oscar confirmed his worst suspicions, the damage would be far greater than last time.

Oscar looked at George's guarded expression. He knew the "pleasure doing business" or "mutual benefit" routines he used with others wouldn't work here. George knew Lando too well, and he understood the cruelty of this industry too well; any evasion would be instantly seen through.

"I'm not looking for an exit strategy," Oscar found the strength to answer firmly. "I'm trying to stay. I just..."

"For what?" George didn't let him off the hook, immediately asking the ultimate question. "You went through all this trouble, slaving away for TVR to torture yourself, and I heard all about it at the Cartier Lawn. The Lotus guys are all debating why that throttle safety dead zone suddenly vanished... I know you did it; there's no other explanation. Why? Why Lando Norris? If you wanted someone to take your obsession out on, why pick him?"

The question hung in the air. Oscar knew he had to lay his cards on the table.

"Because he gave me the keys," he said honestly, not denying the "obsession" accusation. "And only he would give me the keys. Whether it's that Esprit, or the Elise that might get him hurt tomorrow, or himself... he handed the keys to me. You know what that means to him. From the moment we met, he let me sit in the driver's seat of that Esprit. And I don't regret taking those keys, nor will I fall at anyone's feet in gratitude. Because I earned it, and it's what he needs."

He turned his head, thought for a moment, and revealed his final card in this high-stakes game to George.

"I'm not trying to use him to get to some supposedly 'better' place, George. You overestimate me. And what exactly is 'better'? You said the Elise means redemption; I doubt he knows the backstory. He... he chose that car because of me. He came to the hill climb because of me. So I'm not going anywhere... Even though this is incredibly difficult, I'm just one step away."

"And you don't want him to know that yet," George pointed out sharply, looking entirely unmoved.

"He doesn't need to be comforted by that fact." Oscar shook his head. George knew the difficult Oscar Piastri was back. "Actually, if we're talking about who needs it more right now... never mind."

He sniffed, reached out to pat George's shoulder, and gave his body a hard stretch.

"I really have to get back, or I'm going to end up sleeping on cardboard boxes again tonight." He firmly ended the conversation. "Thanks for taking the time out of your precious Cartier Lawn schedule to find me. Do me one last favor: don't be the messenger."

"As long as you don't scurry back to Australia with your tail between your legs like a stray dog," George scoffed. "Mind showing me that Griffith? For a friend's curiosity?"

After thoroughly inspecting the Griffith, George left satisfied, but not before snapping a few photos of the chassis details, causing several engineers to chase after him demanding he delete them, sparking a loud argument. Oscar had to put his noise-canceling headphones back on just to maintain his last shred of focus amidst the chaos. He finally managed to carve out enough time to return to the hotel for a shower, even though the sun was already rising by the time he left the garage. He didn't know if the script he had stayed up all night writing would be perfectly executed, but now he could only leave it to time. He managed a little over an hour of sleep before his alarm vibrated him awake. Dragging his feet out of his room, he passed Lando's door and thought about knocking—just to try—but eventually stopped himself.

Lack of sleep left him feeling chilled to the bone all morning. After briefing the driver on all the key points, he threw on his jacket and huddled in a steel folding chair in the corner of the garage. The parade was first in the afternoon, followed by the F1 showcase, and finally the time attack. The Elise was scheduled to start fifteenth from last, right in the most fiercely competitive zone of the qualifying times. The champion this year was a foregone conclusion: the Ford Supervan had already pulled two or three seconds ahead of second place during qualifying.

Most of the engineers had gone to send the Griffith out onto the track. Oscar, his vision blurring from exhaustion, didn't want to see the car anymore. He shrank into the corner, minimizing his presence, quietly hoping to catch up on some sleep. Years ago in a Silverstone garage, he had also been forced to drive a race car fitted with a motorcycle engine while completely exhausted and freezing; this time, he was only responsible for the strategy, which was a blessing. He really never learns, he thought mockingly, pulling his hood up and closing his eyes. But worrying was useless now, and he quickly fell asleep in the corner.

When the loud chatter woke him up, the Griffith—having just finished its parade duties and polished to a shine—was parked in the center of the garage, surrounded by investors in tall boots and tailored suits. The old-school British TVR engineers were clearly uncomfortable under the capitalist scrutiny, clumsily trying to explain why the car's rear end had slightly fishtailed during a corner.

Oscar took a deep breath, planning to slip away unnoticed and figure out what had happened. He pulled out his phone, found the Griffith in the parade highlights, and realized the "fishtail" they were talking about was an execution error. His freshly woken brain hadn't quite formulated a save yet when a familiar figure in a gray suit smoothly stepped in.

"That fishtail?" It took Oscar a few seconds to recognize the accent of the complaining commercial rep. "That's exactly the 'wildness' we want. A Ford heart, an untamed mustang, but not too cumbersome. Today's sports cars are castrated by electronic systems—you know, the algorithms and technical regulations that make cars boring... But the Griffith was never meant to be like that. It has to bite. It demands to be conquered. That wasn't a loss of control just now; that was the car having a conversation with the driver. Isn't that what the 'character' of British racing is all about? It needs a bit of a temper, otherwise, why not just go to a dealership and buy a Mazda?"

He emphasized the word "temper" perfectly, with a spot-on imitation of Old World pretension. The investors nodded thoughtfully, looking satisfied, as if the technical glitch was proof of noble bloodline. Huddled in the corner, Oscar felt a chill run down his spine; he was so embarrassed he wanted to dig a hole and escape. He checked his phone again. It was time for the F1 parade. Photos of Red Bulls and Mercedes on the track were already refreshing in his feed. The time attack was still ten minutes away.

"I've even seen people modify their headlights to look like crystal chandeliers... You guys are truly fascinating, always mixing and matching. And speaking of mixing and matching, we have to talk about the value of the Griffith's resurrection—if this 1.2-ton Ford V8 can be successfully revived, it will be absolutely beautiful. It will bring back true taste."

Oscar decided to keep holding his breath, taking advantage of the distance between them to pretend he was invisible, leaving everything to his smooth-talking compatriot.

After a while, the crowd dispersed. The commercial rep grabbed a folding chair and walked over to Oscar's corner. He slammed the chair onto the concrete floor, sat down with a heavy exhale, and his smile instantly vanished, looking like he was about to start complaining about everything around him again.

"You almost had me convinced," Oscar offered honestly.

"That's what they want to hear. These Poms—I've been here over six months, and I finally get it. You talk to them about performance stability, and they think you're selling them a kiddie ride. You talk to them about soul and beautiful flaws, and they whip out their checkbooks on the spot." The rep rolled his eyes. "As long as the car doesn't fall apart on the spot, the rest is all storytelling."

"I didn't let it fall apart, did I?" Oscar rubbed his eyes. "I fell asleep just now."

"No, and the performance was actually quite good. The ignition timing logic you wrote is god-tier; that backfire immediately hit the nostalgia sweet spot for those old guys. This is why we need to team up here." The rep pointed at Oscar, then at himself. "You fix the scrap metal full of 'British romance,' and I use the nonsense they understand to sell it right back to them. That's called division of labor."

Oscar couldn't help but laugh. He opened the live stream on his phone. The participant list for the time attack was already on the screen. The commercial rep leaned in curiously to look at the small screen.

"That Ford Supervan is practically cheating, isn't it? 1400 horsepower? What's the point of competing?"

"It left the two Porsche 911 GT3s five seconds in the dust. There's no suspense left."

"Ah, the star car finally shows up..." The rep squinted at the overhead shot of the starting line assembly area. "I didn't realize how small it was yesterday. Is that even half the size of the Supervan? Hopefully, he doesn't almost put it in the wall like yesterday."

"That wasn't 'almost putting it in the wall,'" Oscar corrected him. "That's called using every inch of the track. You might want to use that phrase in the future."

"Huh? Alright..." the rep grunted noncommittally, staring at the screen.

The camera cut to the starting line. A white TWR Supercat had just finished warming its tires right behind the Elise. It was a monster built on a Jaguar XJS base, its wide body taking up almost the entire track, the roar of its supercharged V12 low and heavy. In yesterday's qualifying, it only beat the 210-horsepower little car in front of it by less than 0.3 seconds. Now, even its idling engine sounded anxious.

"Now that's more like it," the rep evaluated. "V12, wide body, armed to the teeth. Pushing for the top ten shouldn't be a problem. Look at this Elise, it looks like a kitten dropped into a wolf den."

"Places ten through fifteen are tight, and power is only part of the equation. Goodwood isn't just about straight lines."

They grabbed two bottles of water from the corner of the garage, gesturing at the cars on screen until the Elise launched. The dynamic driven by the Rover engine was purely mechanical aesthetics. The car, as light as a leaf caught in a typhoon, gripped the asphalt instantly.

"My god..." The water bottle froze in the rep's hand; he completely forgot to drink. "That launch? That grip doesn't look like rear-wheel drive?"

"The power wasn't touched, but the toe angle was tuned very aggressively. Top speed was sacrificed for absolute directional control on launch and through corners."

Before he even finished speaking, Lando had already dove into the Molecomb corner. On screen, the Elise flew past, hugging the inside of the hay bales so closely you couldn't tell if the wheels were still on the ground. Because the car was so light, the uneven surface made it bounce violently, like a cardboard box about to be flipped by a storm. The vibration from the onboard camera made the viewer's wrists ache just watching. The throttle never lifted; it was even smoother than during qualifying. The Elise and its driver turned all the braking distance required by heavier cars into pure speed.

"That maniac... doing all these for a hill climb? Just looking at the steering wheel makes my hands hurt."

"He saved a lot of time on the last two corners, and his handling of Molecomb today was more experienced... or maybe more confident? I think he can break 55 seconds."

"55.65! Unbelievable! " the commentator's voice exploded. "Lando Norris! He pushed this 20-year-old little car under the 55-second mark!"

"He pushed another second faster than qualifying?" The commercial rep almost spilled his water, looking at Oscar—who wore an "I knew it" smile—as if he were an alien. "No way, I don't believe it. Let's watch the Supercat."

The Supercat remained impatient during straight-line acceleration. Its massive momentum forced it to brake heavily much earlier before the corners. The constantly flashing brake lights revealed the driver's over-adjustments and anxiety. The wide body looked cumbersome under the violent weight transfer; the speed lost in those moments was dictated by the laws of physics.

"55.89! Just 0.24 seconds behind! They will be the 10th and 11th if the remained cars do not fall under that line!"

The garage was silent for a second. Then the rep let out a low whistle, his expression complicated.

"Not bad. Lotus is going to go crazy over this. They actually beat a modified monster worth hundreds of thousands of pounds." He turned to Oscar, who was still calmly analyzing the performance of the cars in that interval. "Oh right, in the interview after qualifying yesterday, he said 'his mechanic' tuned it for him... Does Lotus really have someone that good? Someone who can tune an antique to run faster than a V12?"

"There are three cars left with sub-55 second potential. Let me finish watching."

It wasn't until the final Ferrari Roma Coupe slipped out of the 55-second mark due to a launch error that Oscar finally lowered his phone to his lap and took a few gulps of water.

"Actually, no."

"What?" The rep blinked.

"The Lotus engineers gave him a very aggressive modification, but they didn't realize how much of a bonus the technology could provide." Oscar pointed at the replay of the cornering still showing on the screen, a hint of pride in his voice. "They initially left a dead zone in the throttle. They probably aimed for the 60 to 70-second range—less direct feedback, but ensuring the car wouldn't hit the wall. A driver can adapt to it, but psychologically, they can't push to full speed. I was the one who removed that dead zone. I'm the mechanic."

The rep's expression froze. His mouth hung open as his gaze darted back and forth between Oscar and the screen several times.

"You?" the rep lowered his voice. "You changed one thing and made that car break 55 seconds, beating my favorite Supercat, and then you stayed up all night writing the TVR performance script? What is this? Charity?"

"Think of it as... an investment." Oscar shrugged and took another sip of water. "Like I said, we work pretty well together."

"You tech guys have dirty minds. Earning TVR's money while helping a competitor set lap times."

"Well, it's all so I can stay and keep working for you."

"That's true." As his phone vibrated, the rep glanced down. It was an email confirming an electronic signature. "Alright. We've got the TVR letter of intent. The next step is for me to snatch you up for London. Go back and wait for your new contract."

He stood up, patted Oscar on the shoulder, and left the garage. Sitting in the chair, a massive wave of exhaustion washed over Oscar. He hadn't been at the assembly area at the top of the hill, and now he had absolutely no idea how to face Lando—or, more accurately, he didn't even know where to find him. Any form of congratulation felt too frivolous, and he was certain they both felt as if they'd been run over by a truck. He wanted to make a call, but realizing the cars at the top of the hill probably hadn't even come down yet, and not knowing what to say, he ultimately put his phone back in his pocket.

Hesitating wasn't his style, but he had no choice but to slowly chew on his anxiety. Packing up his things, he left the nearly empty garage. Sunday at Goodwood was appropriately sunny. He walked alone from the garage toward the hill climb track, secretly hoping to run into the person he wanted to see as the cars came down the hill. The sun warmed his body back up. He took off his jacket, stuffed it in his bag, and only then realized he was still wearing the purple TVR pass around his neck.

He thought for a second, then took off the pass and shoved it in his bag too.

The entrance to the hill climb track was already packed with media. Every car coming down was stopped for a quick interview before the driver was escorted back to the garage by the marketing department. Oscar stood on his tiptoes on the periphery, peering into the crowd, but he couldn't see a thing. His stomach growled. He hadn't eaten anything since a quick bite after showering at the hotel that morning. The low blood sugar was probably why his vision had gone black in the TVR garage and forced him to sleep. Giving up on squeezing through the crowd, he found a food truck about to close, bought a Greek gyro, and sat on a bench in a corner of the estate. Under the vendor's pitying gaze, he devoured it in just a few bites.

"Want another?" the vendor asked hesitantly, taking in his appearance. "You look like you just crawled out of hell."

He considered it and decided to dig into his pocket for some change. But the vendor stopped him.

"I'm about to leave anyway. Have the last bit on the house."

"Thanks..."

He ate the second gyro very slowly. After bidding the vendor farewell, he continued eating while pulling out his phone to check the final Goodwood schedule. In the evening sun, he watched the surrounding lights gradually turn on. He opened Instagram. Lando still hadn't posted anything, though his studio account had posted at least fifty photos from the track. George, however, had posted a story—a selfie with a taller, middle-aged man. Lando appeared to be near the display booth in the background.

He swallowed the last bite of food, tossed the wrapper into a nearby bin, and went to dig out the purple pass. Halfway through, he remembered it only said "Mechanic" and couldn't get him onto that lawn. He looked at himself: wearing a T-shirt and sneakers, undoubtedly covered in dust, carrying a large backpack. There was no way he was getting in legitimately.

He had spent four whole days at Goodwood. He hadn't just been fixing cars; he had mapped out the entire estate in his head. He remembered a section of fencing hidden by azalea bushes near the cricket pitch. The shrubs were so overgrown that the top of the wire fence had been bent down, leaving it only about 20 centimeters taller than him. For a creepy stalker who had just crawled out of hell, a slightly lower wall was a perfectly valid entrance.

He pretended to casually stroll by while observing the area. He walked past the white fences and security guards, bypassed the noisy crowds, his shoe soles crunching softly on the damp fallen leaves—a sound masked by the distant jazz music. The massive azalea bush was bright and served as a natural screen. Oscar looked left and right, confirmed no one was around, took off his backpack, and took a deep breath.

He didn't care anymore. He tossed his backpack over first, stepped onto a horizontally growing tree trunk, and gripped the wire at the top of the fence with both hands. The cold touch of the metal sharpened his senses. He pulled himself up, his muscles screaming in protest from the day's exhaustion, but he gritted his teeth and clumsily but effectively hoisted himself over. As he swung his leg, his trousers caught on a protruding wire, making a soft tearing sound. His center of gravity shifted, and he tumbled over the fence, landing flat on his back on the grass.

He lay there panting for a few seconds before scrambling up. He grabbed his backpack, hid in the shadow of a massive oak tree, and began scoping out the layout inside. White tents, elegant long tables. People in silk and bespoke suits holding champagne, laughing and chatting under the soft lighting. Near the edge were glass display cases, relatively quiet, with only a few people examining the exhibits.

Just as he was trying to map out a stealthy search route, he spotted Lando near the display cases. He had changed into a shirt and dress pants and was closely examining an intricately patterned porcelain vase. A staff member approached him, seemingly explaining the history of the piece. Oscar originally intended to wait for Lando to leave the area before rushing over, but the man kept talking endlessly.

I'm going to be seen anyway, Oscar thought. He stepped out of the shadows and walked straight toward them. He saw Lando actually picking up the vase as the staff member described the Herend porcelain*. That was a terrible idea, especially if Lando's hands were still shaking. Before his voice could draw attention, Oscar reached out—to the shock of both—and took the vase from Lando's hands, placing it safely back on the display stand.

"And this is..."

"Ah... he came in with me. Sorry."

Lando quickly waved the staff off, grabbed the clearly trespassing Oscar by the arm, and dragged him behind a white tent.

"How did you get yourself into the lawn?" Lando picked a few blades of grass out of Oscar's hair. "Where did you come from? I thought Mark was bringing you in."

"Can we go outside first? I have something to tell you."

"Through the front gate?"

"They shouldn't care, right? We're leaving, not entering."

Lando clearly hadn't processed the situation yet, but he complied. Ignoring the security guards' suspicious glares, they walked out the gates of the Cartier Lawn as if nothing had happened and turned toward a nearby bench that was decently lit. Some hay bales hadn't been cleared away yet, providing a bit of cover.

"Too much has happened..." Oscar scratched his head. "I have to tell you one by one. I'm really sorry I didn't go up the hill to find you today... I actually had time, but..."

"It's fine." Lando interrupted him. "Didn't I say I don't blame you?"

"55 seconds 65...! I knew you could break 55. I saw your last two corners and knew you had it in the bag."

Lando looked down and smiled, shyly rubbing his nose.

"Second thing... wait, I think there are only two things, but it is too complicated. Never mind." He pulled out his nearly dead phone, pulled up the newly submitted transfer application email, and shoved it into Lando's hands. "I wasn't doing charity work for TVR. I got this."

Lando held the phone, staring blankly at the lines of text on the screen, completely stunned. He indeed didn't make much of a scene. When he looked up, his eyes were full of disbelief, and then large teardrops suddenly began streaming down his face, splashing onto the darkened screen and his hands. Oscar quickly reached out to wipe them away, but the tears just kept coming.

"But... but you're still doing things you don't like..." Lando sobbed. "Didn't I say this was just the first step... later..."

"You never said how big this first step had to be." Oscar took the phone from his hands, slipped it back into his pocket, and tightly gripped both of Lando's hands as Lando tried to wipe his tears with both arms. "Besides, I think I'm actually pretty good at doing them."

"I lied to you from the very beginning..." Lando seemed entirely unable to hear him, tears falling freely, looking like he might hyperventilate at any moment, yet his hands gripped Oscar's tightly. "It was just a spur-of-the-moment thing. I didn't even have Glastonbury tickets... And I did that stupid necklace thing..."

As he spoke, realizing both his hands were occupied, he simply used his sleeve to wipe the tears and snot off his face. Oscar was now certain he could smell the pungent scent of muscle rub beneath the saltiness of Lando's tears.

"And at Glasto... it was just because I was scared. I couldn't take it anymore, so I dragged you back to the tent to have sex... We missed the entire Raye set!"

"If you hadn't brought it up, I wouldn't have even thought about that..."

"You should have! Weren't you at least a little bit there for her?" Lando suddenly raised his voice in protest, prompting Oscar to quickly check if anyone was coming. Realizing he was too loud, Lando's posture, which had just straightened, slumped back down. He suddenly let go of Oscar's hands and began patting himself down.

"The worst part is this..." He pulled an orange visitor pass out of his pocket. The small card read 'Guest - All Access'. "I applied for this as soon as you decided to come to Goodwood. But I didn't want to make you angry, and I was terrified you wouldn't be able to get in..."

Oscar took the card with both hands, his own eyes starting to sting. After Glasto, he had thought Lando wouldn't do something like this anymore, given everything he had said. But Lando had still applied for a safety net for him. He belatedly understood Lando's overreaction to TVR around the Silverstone test. Lando had kept this pass safe all this time just to protect his pride. He watched as Lando gradually calmed down, wiping his face with trembling hands, his eyes swollen beyond recognition. Oscar experienced a rare failure of words, which finally condensed into one abrupt, contextless sentence:

"Let's run away."

"...Where to?" Lando stopped wiping his face, looking at him with a mix of confusion and complaint.

"Um... I don't know. But you can't exactly go back to the lawn looking like this, can you? And I feel like this hay bale smells weird... Where do you want to go right now?"

"I don't know, my head hurts... Only my back hurt before, but now my head hurts too..."

"I know there's a shortcut exit near the garages. The media will never find it, we just have to take a bit of a detour. If you want to go back to the hotel to rest, we can go that way. That's how I got back to the hotel this morning."

"This morning? Got back to the hotel?"

"Uh, we can talk about that later."

"Actually, you can use this pass to get onto the Cartier Lawn. There are a lot of things inside you'd definitely want to see..." Lando pointed at the small card in Oscar's hand, making Oscar laugh through his tears.

"It's the same every year, isn't it? I'll see it later." Meeting Lando's pitiful gaze, Oscar shoved the pass into his own pocket. "Or should we go get something to eat? If there are too many people we know here, we can drive further away. Or, if you want to go back and have sex again, we can do that too."

Hearing this, Lando looked like he was about to burst into another round of tears while wildly shaking his head.

"You don't want to leave?"

"Every part hurts so much..." Lando's voice was muffled, still thick with unshed tears, heartbreakingly honest. "It hurts too much. I don't want to have sex right now."

"Do you want to go back now, then?" Oscar wasn't sure how much of what he said was registering, so he tried to solve the immediate problem, reaching out to massage Lando's stiff shoulders. "Just lying around the room?"

"Are you going back to Melbourne in a couple of days?" Lando nodded, then suddenly remembered.

"Yeah... I have to go back this time. There's some paperwork to sort out, I don't know how long it will take."

"I'm going too. I've worked so hard for so long, I want a vacation."

"You mean you want to vacation in a place where it's currently winter. The wind will cause depression."

"I'm going on vacation. That's not my problem."

After saying that, Lando wiped his face again. Staring at the nearby hay bales, he caught his breath, then tugged at his ruined shirt. It seemed as though if he looked at Oscar's face for one more second, his tear ducts would lose control again, so he kept his head down and refused to speak. The jazz music from the lawn was so elegant, mixed with high-minded conversation that surely included praise for the 55.65-second miracle, and speculation about the mysterious mechanic who dared tamper with Lotus's display car. But to them, it all sounded like a cold eviction notice. Evading the security guards and VIPs around the lawn, they slipped quietly into the woods, escaping from this weekend filled with speed, noise, and vanity.

"Woking... Why Woking?"

Lando stood by the window of the new apartment, looking helplessly at the gray sky outside and a row of even grayer houses. Behind him, Oscar was carefully using scissors to cut open the packaging of a spring mattress, wary of being launched across the room by the massive object if he wasn't careful.

"The only thing you'll be able to eat here is Pizza Express. Look at the people on the street, not a single one is wearing color... Imagine our Esprit parked downstairs. It would look like a kid stuck a glittery sticker on a black-and-white photograph."

"It's only a 20-minute drive from Heathrow, it's convenient to get to central London, and the rent is reasonable." Oscar finally finished his battle with the mattress, successfully landing it on the bed frame. "And there are a lot of manufacturers here."

"Like who?"

Oscar joined him at the window, following Lando's pointing finger. In the distance, an alien spaceship-like building was visible. It took him a few seconds to realize it was McLaren's headquarters.

"You didn't pick this apartment for the view, did you?"

"I swear to God I didn't," Oscar quickly denied. "Besides, I was at home when I picked this place. You were the one who came to see it for me."

"I was obviously looking only at the interior! Why would I pay attention to what's in the distance outside the window?"

"Do you have a problem with it?"

"I just feel like it gives me this inexplicable sense of... pressure? I'm installing blackout curtains on this window today." Lando thought about it. "I've toured the inside. The floors are so shiny you can see your face in them. Are you trying to get a job there?"

"At the very least, they wouldn't want me right now. There's no need to think that far ahead," Oscar shrugged, turning back to unpack the cardboard boxes on the floor. "Come help me."

"Speaking of which..." Lando took one last look at the MTC building before turning around and sitting on the floor, starting to unpack his luggage item by item. "Seeing the MTC just made me realize I actually can't remember."

"Can't remember what?"

"Who is driving the F1 car for McLaren right now?"
