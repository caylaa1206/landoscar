POST FP1 DEBRIEF

Engineers were shuffling about the room, their necks ringed by bulky headsets and arms stuffed full of messy folders. They were chattering in a language Lando didn’t fully understand-- even after all these years, scribbling similarly cryptic notes onto Post-Its. Lando was used to engineers looking a little frantic, hair askew, trailing loose paper and a scent of burned rubber wherever they went.

That was why, when Oscar walked into the meeting room with nothing in his hands, Lando felt a pit in his stomach open.

Jesus, was he stuck with one of those engineers? Who didn’t give enough of a shit to even bring his Free Practice notes to a meeting on... well, the Free Practice?

Lando had already had some qualms about Oscar Piastri. The first thing that had struck him, when Andrea forwarded the guy’s application to his email, was how young he was. Sure, he wasn’t actually much younger than Lando, but weren’t engineers supposed to be sort of... old? With, like, a lot of degrees and experience?

Seemingly, though, Oscar ticked every box. His engineering thesis was apparently brilliant: a delve into some convoluted aero-balance maths. Teams had been scouting him since his second year. Alpine almost snagged him, which led to a brief legal scuffle that put Zak in a foul mood for a few months. But, Mclaren had fought for Oscar to shocking lengths. Lando still remembered how Zak had described him in that final meeting,

“With him on your side, you’ll get what you really want, Lando.”

He hadn’t had to say it. Everyone knew what Lando was hungry for: that first win. He hunted the glory of it in restless strides, his hands always stretched forward, searching for, grasping for. So far his fingers had curled over nothing but air, no matter how maddeningly close he got. He still prickled sometimes when it began to rain, memories of Sochi gnawing at him. The failure was eating him alive– and so he’d signed off on Oscar Piastri. It was a cheap trick, saying that some 22 year old bloke could get him the win, but it worked. Lando couldn’t resist. He'd always had bad impulse control.

But now everything was definitely, absolutely backfiring because Oscar had shown up empty fucking handed, didn’t even seem particularly bothered about it and–

“Ah, sorry. My laptop’s under your jacket. Could you just..?”

Lando jumped, Oscar was standing above him, gesturing at a slim laptop under Lando’s hoodie.

“Shit, sorry mate. Here.”

Oscar gave him a slight smile and sat across from him, immediately opening his laptop and producing a notepad from his pocket. His fingers began to dance rapidly across the keyboard, and Lando felt like an idiot. Of course Oscar had been taking notes. Lando needed to chill the fuck out. He wanted to ask why Oscar’s laptop had been stranded at the engineer table, why he took notes on paper like some eighty year old man, but then Daniel Ricciardo walked in, laughing with his engineer, and that was the cue for the debrief to start.

Andrea cleared his throat, and the engineer’s rippling conversations died. Everyone looked to Andrea expectantly, but he just motioned towards the Head of Engineering, Rob. Rob gave a tight smile, “Good work so far, guys. We still have those tricky areas, but we knew that that would be the case. Right now is more for the drivers and pit wall, but we’ll be covering more in our mechanics meeting later.”

Lando refused to be the first person to speak, but apparently Oscar had no qualms. He flipped to a specific page in his little notepad and looked up,

“Like Rob said, we knew the last turn wouldn’t be great, but the grip is failing every time. Um, I’d need Tom to jump in here too, but I’ve had Lando on Purple C2 and C3, tried second and third, and rotated through tyres. Nothing is changing. Tom? Daniel?”

Tom Stallard, Daniel’s race engineer, looked up and frowned, eyes crinkling, “Yep, we’ve been trying every mode. Nothing’s working. I think there’s a possibility, honestly a big one, that someone spins out.”

Lando felt like he should probably finally speak, “Yeah, I’ve no grip like, the whole way through. I almost lost it on the new hards.”

He looked up from where he was tugging on a knotted bracelet, and met Oscar’s tilted gaze. He hadn’t said that to him, on the radio– that he’d almost binned it. Lando looked away, but not before he caught Oscar bending to scribble something in his notes.

In the background, Daniel was complaining about the opening straight, and Lando groaned in agreement. The two of them threw their frustrations at the table, and sat back to half heartedly listen as the engineers began to pick at the threads. Oscar barely said anything for the rest of the meeting, but Lando could tell, somehow, that he was paying close attention. He had a very distinct focused face, lips settled into a perfect straight line, eyebrows drawing down over low-lidded eyes. Lando felt himself frowning. Max had sent him stupid candids of him focusing, really focusing– like when he was trying to write something entirely right for posts or articulate something the right way, and his expression always drew into itself, creases and folds marring his skin like fault lines. Oscar’s focus was gentle but driven, Lando’s always looked like anger.

Lando decided that this was an unhelpful and useless train of thought, so he abandoned it in favor of complaining about Turn 15 some more.

RACE SUNDAY, 9:00AM

Lando stood in the morning sun’s pale streams, armed with a Sharpie and a fuckton of willpower. He signed until his wrist hurt, trying to smile, to keep himself from squinting angrily from the heat. People were swarming, but he was used to that now. He let beaded bracelets drop into his palms, hugged younger fans, and attacked mini helmets with his marker. Eventually Charlotte dragged him away, tutting at him. She ushered him into McLaren Hospitality, instructed him to sit down, eat, and wait for Jon, and then she walked pointedly away.

Lando always struggled to eat on race mornings, but he grabbed a bowl of oatmeal and a banana, praying Jon would take mercy on him and his protein intake. While he waited, he picked at a particularly vicious hangnail. He’d been trying to kick the habit since he was a kid, but it didn’t seem to be going anywhere anytime soon. Especially not at the start of a season.

He ended every race weekend with nails bitten to the quick, the skin surrounding them gnawed at until it bled. People noticed, obviously, but he just couldn’t stop.

Lando looked up at the sound of approaching footsteps and groaned immediately. It was Jon, armed with a protein shake and a plate stacked with protein pancakes. Lando was going to throw up on him to prove a point. A person could only handle so much protein

After choking down inhuman amounts of protein, Lando escaped to his Driver Room. He always felt safe here. It was pretty cramped, but the low lighting and utter privacy made it almost cozy. Lando shuffled over to the rack where his suit hung and slipped his sweater off, donning sweltering layer of fireproof upon fireproof, and finally, the glaring papaya-orange suit. Lando knew, objectively, that it was ugly but he harbored a strange sort of fondness for it. 

When he was done changing, Lando grabbed his fluro resistance bands and flung the door open to go find Jon.

“Oscar?”

His engineer was standing directly outside his door, face twisted in confusion.

“Oh. So I take it that this isn’t a bathroom then.”

Lando stared at him incredulously, and then burst into laughter, “No. Thought the ‘Driver Room’ sign would have tipped you off.”

He watched as Oscar squinted at the door, eyes finding the silver plaque. He smiled, still just a subtle, sheepish twitch of his mouth, and turned to leave, “Sorry, mate. See you later. Or– hear you later.”

Lando scoffed, “You’ll be seeing me for sure. When I take the team out for a celebratory dinner.”

Oscar paused in the hallway and raised an eyebrow, smile growing, “Oh yeah? What will we be celebrating?”

Lando gave an exaggerated shrug, “Not sure yet. We’ll see.”

Oscar’s eyes crinkled and he said, soft, “Alright-y then. Uh, sorry again.”

Lando watched him walk away, feet fused to the floor. He looked down at the resistance bands in his hands. Why the fuck was he holding those? He shook his head in confusion and retreated back into his room. He didn’t remember that he was meeting Jon to stretch until thirty minutes later, at which point his phone had racked up four missed calls and several very frighteningly punctuated WhatsApp messages.

THE MTC, 8:00 AM

Lando tapped his keycard on the reader at the sprawling, glass doors of the MTC, humming tunelessly. His stomach was a knot of energy and a restless wanting.  He had to sit through another race debrief before he could get to what he was really there for: the simulator.

Lando knew from other drivers that most people hated simwork. They wanted the reserves to do it, they wanted their engineers to leave them alone, they just wanted the real thing. And, yeah, nothing beat the scream of tyres on tarmac, but the simulator was a ladder to the podium. Lando loved running the same corners over and over, adjusting the specs ever-so-slightly, pushing the brakes in different ways, pushing himself  in different ways. 

Lando was far from a perfectionist in his everyday life— his flat was a fucking mess (once, when he was 19, Max burst in armed with bleach wipes and a mop), he still didn’t fully understand how to do his washing, and he had no qualms with sending a text message riddled with spelling errors. But, but, racing was different. The car was his sanctuary, and he was a revenant. He prayed to it with hours on the simulator, hours in bed watching replays, hours of dreams about rain-soaked losses. 

Lando made sure to say hello to everyone as he walked through the main floor, bumping fists with the engineers he knew well, offering polite smiles to the ones he didn’t. He always felt like a bit of a dick, swanning in here with something new to complain about each week. 

But if they fixed it, he’d win.

He was hungry. Always. 

 

RACE DEBRIEF, 8:45 AM

“Right, guys, I hope you all enjoyed my silence during the weekend, because there’ll be none of that now,” Rob smiled down the table, and a few of the engineers chuckled. Oscar, Lando noticed, stayed silent, eyebrows furrowing as he flipped through stacks of notes. He was sitting rather far away, which didn’t matter, but Lando was used to sitting next to his engineers. He liked the security, the weight of a familiar voice at his ear. Tom and Daniel were next to each other. Whatever. 

Rob had prepared a PowerPoint, á la George Russell, and spoke through all the different engine modes and how they were working with different downforce adjustments, how the tyres were performing or struggling, how the drivers could adjust, how the engineers could adjust. It was... a lot.

They watched Lando’s first lap, talked through it. Watched Daniel’s first lap, talked through that. Then they watched them side by side, and Lando and Daniel fell relatively silent as Oscar and Tom compared the performances of the red A11 versus the purple C2 on top of the different tyre combos they’d taken. Daniel had started on hards, which made Lando wince to think about even now. 

“Honestly, focus is still on Turn 15 and that first straight. Straight line speed was always going to be a struggle, but it needs to be addressed before Shanghai, or we’ve got no chance.”

Lando listened as Oscar spoke-- it was actually sort of odd hearing him speak in full sentences. Lando was used to monosyllabic sound bites, quick warnings or suggestions. It was weird actually seeing him, too. He was perfectly still while he spoke, but as he listened he would come alive, toying with a pen, twirling it in his fingers, or scribbling in the margins of his notepad. Lando wondered if he did the same on the pitwall, messing with a pen as Lando’s crackly voice flooded his headset. It would have been even weirder seeing him without the headset, except he had donned a garishly bright McLaren cap for the debrief. Lando wondered idly if he'd ever see him without the headset or a hat. Not that it mattered.

They went round in circles talking about Bahrain, and then unceremoniously shifted to Jeddah. They concluded that setup was the big focus, but that Lando and Daniel needed to manage expectations. Their results would likely be similar.

POST QUALIFYING DEBRIEF, 6:00 PM

He could feel his chest tightening as he walked out of the meeting room, his mind starting to race. “Lando! Sorry, mate, wait up a second!”

Lando turned, eyebrow quirking. Oscar was speed-walking down the hallway, clutching a thermos of coffee. Lando waited until he’d caught up, and gave an exaggerated flick of his hand.

Well, go on. 

“I just wanted to say, erm– you’re right to be concerned about the balance. Ah, that sounds shit, I just mean, I’m concerned too. And I’m working on it. And, uh, if you have any worries that you feel like you can’t voice during the meetings or on the radio or whatever, Charlotte’s sent you an email with my number.”

Lando stared at Oscar, opening and closing his mouth. He was too tired to deal with people being nice to him, but he was still a little struck by it. 

“I- thanks, mate. I’ll probably take you up on that,” He coughed awkwardly, “You might have noticed that I’m a bit of a worrier.”

Oscar flashed him a crooked smile, “You just care a lot. It’s okay– good, even. I care a lot, too.”

“Yeah, I can tell,” Lando said, voice rough, “Thanks, seriously.”

Oscar gave him an awkwards thumbs up and began to back away, “Sure. Any question is good. Or input. Anything. Well maybe not anything but– yeah. Catch you later, mate.”

Lando lifted his hand in a wordless goodbye, and watched as Oscar disappeared down the dark hallway. As soon as he was gone, it was hard to remember that he’d ever been there at all, like some odd mirage. 

 

HOTEL ROOM, JEDDAH CORNICHE CIRCUIT, 1:00 AM
